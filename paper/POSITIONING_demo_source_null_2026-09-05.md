## 0. The finding being positioned
- One corrected world, one rnd30 file, LAST checkpoints, exact permutation tests, registered predictions. Random-start pick: DP (pruned human v machine) 0.520 v 0.467, p .12, 10v10; RLPD (raw human v machine) 0.600 v 0.517, p .49, 8v8 (one dead seed per arm); r2dreamer (raw human v machine) 0.617 v 0.608, p .88, 8v8; on 300 starts 0.602 v 0.618, p .55. All-data human arm (+40 failed/non-picking tapes): no gain (WM 0.588 v 0.617; RLPD 0.554 v 0.600). DP on raw human 0.237 — pruning (+0.27) dwarfs source (+0.05).
- Machine demos = rollouts of a DP teacher trained on the pruned human set, success-filtered, IC-matched: a *distillation* of the human data, not an independent generator. Raw human tapes: 45% idle decisions v 4%, identical approach geometry, humans faster when moving (d_z ≈ 1), +6 s to grasp.
- Caveats: one task, sim only, n 8–10, learner-specific budgets, WM recipe fixed on the pruned pair, and an old-world RLPD +0.21 (p .001) that did not survive the corrected world (A20: −0.02, p .98).

## 2. Verdict — is the null "broadly understood"?
**BC / idle time — yes, established.** Diffusion Policy calls idle removal "common practice"; ACT, robomimic (history helps on human data) and the 2026 standardisation paper agree. Our contribution is the like-to-like quantification (idle 45% v 4%, d_z 3.4; pruning +0.27 v source +0.05 in one design) and that the *same* raw tapes that break DP are harmless to the other two learners. Re-confirmation; cite the four.
**RLfD indifference to source — folk-expected, not directly evidenced.** Every RLfD paper claims robustness to demo *quality* (NAC "arbitrary quality", RLPD "sub-optimal trajectories", MCAC, JSRL's coverage theory) and the papers mix sources across benchmarks without comparing (IBRL, RLPD's Adroit buffer, DreamerFD). The two studies that did compare sources for a value learner point in *different* directions — robomimic (offline: machine fine, human hard) and Orsini (AIL: synthetic misleads) — and neither is like-to-like or online. Our cell is the first controlled online one we found, but a reviewer will read it as "what everyone assumed", and it is the weakest of our three (p .49, one dead seed per arm, large MDE at n=8).
**World models — one precedent, MoDem.** MoDem Fig. 8 already reports same-asymptote learning from human v near-optimal-policy demos for a model-based learner (Adroit, 5 seeds, no statistics). r2dreamer replicates that in a different family (Dreamer-style imagination actor-critic) with distillation-generated machine demos, pre-registered, n=8, 30 and 300 random starts, and adds the raw-tape and all-data axes MoDem lacks. Novel: the tight null (per-seed spread ≈2/30, so a 0.10 gap was detectable), the failed-tape null, and the same-demos-three-learners factorization. Not novel: "world models don't care who made the demos" — cite MoDem as precedent; do not claim "first".
**Failures don't help — new but weak.** Kumar 2022 predicts value learners profit from suboptimal/failed data offline. Our online learners did not (WM −0.03, RLPD −0.05, both n.s.). The candidate mechanism — an online learner manufactures its own failures, so demo failures are redundant, whereas an offline learner cannot — is untested, and 40 tapes is a small dose. Report as a registered prediction not met, not as a law.

## 3. Framing for publication
**F1 — "Source is second-order; preprocessing and coverage are first-order."** Within DP, idle pruning (+0.27) dwarfs source (+0.05); within RLPD, the earlier +0.21 human advantage tracked the machine set's end-effector coverage deficit (−21% old world v −5% corrected; figs 6/7 per the `RELATED_WORK_TOP10` addendum) and vanished with it. One variable — *which states the demonstrator visits* — reconciles robomimic (broad-explorer machine data helps value learners), RoboCasa/MimicGen (transform-generated data matches human for BC) and our null (coverage-matched distilled data is interchangeable). Strongest framing; needs the coverage figure in the main text and the old-world result as a disclosed sensitivity row (A34).
**F2 — "A pre-registered, like-to-like, three-learner null with a mechanism."** Same tapes, world, eval file and statistic; the only descriptor separating the sets is time structure (idle, speed when moving), not geometry; the only learner that reads time structure as a regression target (BC) is the only one that reacts; RL/WM consume demos as state coverage plus a terminal reward, which distillation preserves. Defensible, but confirmatory-sounding unless F1's coverage story or a second task is attached.
**F3 — "Evaluate on the demo source you will deploy with — but the warning is learner-class dependent."** Extends Orsini 2021 and robomimic: recipe conclusions transfer across demo sources for RLfD and WM in our data and do not for BC without idle pruning. Modest, methodological; workshop-sized or a short section.
**Strongest reviewer objections (answer in this order).** (1) Sim-only, one ~16–20 s pick with a 17-dim privileged state — the idle/non-Markov pathology is worst for pixel policies, so the BC arm may understate it and the RL/WM null may not transfer to images. (2) n=8–10: state the minimum detectable effect per arm (WM ≈0.10; RLPD ≈0.3–0.4 per the A20 note) and add an equivalence test (TOST, registered margin) rather than a failed rejection. (3) Budget units differ per learner (gradient steps v decisions v sim steps); no ranking is claimed — say so in the caption. (4) The teacher was trained on the same human data: a *distillation* null (D4RL "cloned" is the precedent), not a machine-data null; an independent generator is needed before generalising. (5) The old-world +0.21 (p .001) that vanished invites a forking-paths reading unless both worlds sit in one table with the coverage covariate. (6) WM recipe fixed on the pruned pair from the same recordings — tuning leakage; the raw set was untouched but the machine set was not. (7) Set-size asymmetry (66 v 58 tapes, 14.5k v 7.5k rows) inside the RL/WM rows; only DP is size-matched. (8) Success-filtered machine demos v unfiltered human successes; the 11 hardest ICs exist only on the human side. (9) dv3 excluded — one WM port, not "world models". (10) Deterministic v sampled evaluation across learners.
**Evidence that would most raise the bar.** (a) A second task family: the place phase is in flight (`PHASE_RESULTS_2026-09-05.md`); cheaper and more citable is RLPD/r2dreamer on robomimic Can/Square with the public PH v MG sets, which speaks directly to the canonical study. (b) An independent machine generator (RL-trained teacher, or a MimicGen-style transform) to separate "distillation" from "machine". (c) A quality axis wider than idle: robomimic-style Worse/Okay/Better operators, or the registered A28 retimed/smoothed/noised packs — turns a null into a dose–response. (d) A real-robot leg for one learner (HIL-SERL scale: 20–30 demos, 1–2.5 h per policy). (e) A pixel-input arm, where the idle mechanism is strongest. (f) Power: registered equivalence margins and 12–16 seeds for the RLPD row, whose null is currently carried by dead seeds. (g) Publish the old-world/corrected-world pair as a 2×3 table with EEF coverage as covariate — it is the paper's most interesting object, not an embarrassment.

## 4. Summary (10 lines)
1. BC's sensitivity to idle time in raw human teleop is established (Diffusion Policy §4.3/§6.1, ACT, robomimic, 2026 standardisation); our +0.27 pruning effect is a re-confirmation.
2. RLfD source-indifference is universally assumed (NAC, RLPD, MCAC, JSRL) but no prior like-to-like human-v-machine online cell was found; the two offline/AIL comparisons that exist point in opposite directions.
3. For world models, MoDem (2022) already reports same-asymptote learning from human v policy demos; cite it and do not claim "first".
4. New here: same tapes × three learners, distillation-generated machine set, IC-matched, pre-registered, 30+300 random starts, plus the raw-tape and failed-tape axes.
5. The tight null is the WM one (0.617 v 0.608, spread ≈2/30); the RLPD null is weak (p .49, dead seeds); the DP contrast is small and n.s.
6. Failed human tapes gave no gain to either online learner — a registered prediction not met; the "online learners make their own failures" mechanism is a hypothesis.
7. Recommended framing: F1 — source is second-order once the world is right; idle pruning (BC) and state coverage (value learners) are first-order — with the two-world coverage table in the main text.
8. Fatal if unaddressed: distillation ≠ machine data; sim-only single task; the vanished old-world effect; per-learner budgets.
9. Cheapest bar-raisers: robomimic PH v MG under RLPD/r2dreamer; an independent generator; equivalence tests with registered margins; the place phase.
10. As it stands: a solid section of the human-v-machine paper or a workshop negative result; a standalone paper only with (a)+(b) from §3.
