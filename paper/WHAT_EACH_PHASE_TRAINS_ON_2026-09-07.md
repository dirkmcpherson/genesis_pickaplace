## The problem, stated plainly

**Slide is the only phase where the reward and the score disagree.** The environment pays and terminates on bare `contact`, which a policy can collect while still gripping the can and pressing it into the goal. We then score `slide_success`, which requires the can to be standing on the shelf, released. A policy that maximises the reward has **no incentive to open the gripper** — releasing can only lose it the episode.

This is not speculation; it is already measured:
- Carrycontact re-score, human seed 1: `contact` 124/148, of which only 27 pass the geometric push test (86 fail wrong-side), and **`slide_success` 0/148**.
- Slide-phase smokes, untrained policies: bare `contact` 5/11, 7/11, 2/11, **`slide_success` 0/11**, with the per-clause reason `grip_closed` on every checked episode.

So as currently configured, the 32 Slide runs would spend ~130 GPU-hours learning to drive a held can into the goal, and would then be scored on a criterion they were never trained for. Expected result: 0 for both arms, a null that means nothing.

**I have therefore put all 32 Slide jobs on hold** (`sl_rlpd_*` 3355056–71, `sl_dp_*` 3355072–87 — held, not cancelled; releasing them is one command). Pick, Place, the end-to-end arms, the robomimic recovery and the dv3 comparison are unaffected and still running.

## The three ways out (your call)

1. **Reward what we score.** Move the Slide grant from bare `contact` to the `slide_success` clauses: do not terminate on contact while the gripper is closed; pay and terminate when the can is in contact, released and standing. Cost: an env change plus a re-registration; the phase then trains the behaviour we care about. My recommendation.
2. **Keep the reward, rename the phase.** Accept that this phase is "bring the can into contact by any means", report `slide_success` only as a diagnostic, and stop calling it Slide. Cheapest, but it does not answer the question you asked for.
3. **Two-stage reward.** Small positive at contact, larger at release-with-contact, so the policy is guided but must release to finish. Most likely to actually learn, but it introduces reward shaping into a project whose results all rest on sparse rewards, and would need its own control.
