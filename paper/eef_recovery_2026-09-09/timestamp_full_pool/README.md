## Playback approximation and stage regressions

The paired summary now reports all failure-stage changes, not only transitions
into or out of complete. At the first 31 finished trials, 244 and 259 lose upright
release and 246 loses pickup, while 237 gains final retention. The absence of lost
complete sequences at that point must not be described as no regressions overall.

The 30 ms timestamp grid also interpolates the measured joint/grip samples. A
round-trip diagnostic (resampling back to original source times) finds a maximum
joint discrepancy of 0.0111 rad across the pool, about 0.636 degrees. Trial 237's
maximum is 0.00820 rad. See resampling_roundtrip.json for every trial. This measures
information loss on the command grid, not actual arm tracking error. Consequently,
these results test timestamp reconstruction with interpolation together; they do
not isolate timing from path resampling. The bit-identical independent EEF replay
checks reproducibility of that reconstruction, not exact preservation of every
original measurement or validation of the physical hand model.

Trial 317 is also independently verified and packaged in the timestamp manifest.
Trajectory, EEF actions and observations reproduce exactly; six selected phase
frames show upright release, later slide into contact and hand separation. Its
fixed-clock control fails after release. The changed playback includes interpolation;
this is not a calibrated physical-fidelity claim. See 317/paired_sequence.json.
