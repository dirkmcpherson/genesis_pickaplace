## 2. What changed in three days, in one paragraph

We discovered that the headline result the paper was built on — model demos beat human demos for
BC, 0.96 vs 0.62 — was an artifact of how human demos were translated into the learners' action
space. Rebuilding the pipeline so every demo source is *recorded as executed* through one recorder
in the learners' own MDP made the gap vanish. Along the way the simulator turned out to have three
real defects (no arm gravity compensation, a shelf modelled half-buried, and a contact-solver
timeconstant 8× the engine's stability floor), two of which were cancelling each other out. The
project's claim has moved from "human vs model demonstrations" toward "apparent demo-source effects
are dominated by translation and simulation artifacts; what survives is learner-specific."

## 3. Results, with their caveats

All numbers: mean over seeds of the selected checkpoint. `hold` = 15 demo ICs, `rnd` = 30 random
ICs. Regenerate with `analysis/results_table.py`.

### 3a. Diffusion policy (BC)

| world | source | hold | rnd | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| old (`matched_v2`) | dR2D | 0.83 | **0.61** | 5 |
| old | dH | 0.81 | 0.57 | 5 |
| old | dDP | 0.89 | 0.55 | 5 |
| corrected (`matched_w3`) | dH | 0.91 | **0.55** | 5 |
| corrected | dDP | 0.85 | 0.49 | 5 |

**Caveat that changes the reading (N11):** `hold` is ceiling-limited — no DP run in 22 attempts has
ever exceeded 14/15, and the `sel` set contains an IC (uid 234, lying can) that is unachievable by
construction: picked 0 times in 430 policy-evals. On `hold` the dH-vs-dDP ordering *flips* between
worlds, which is what ceiling noise looks like. On `rnd`, which has real headroom, there is a small
but consistent ordering: **dDP is lowest in both worlds, dR2D best where it exists** (gaps 0.02–0.06,
n=5). So "source parity for BC" is defensible only as "no effect resolvable at n=5 on `rnd`" —
not as "no effect." DP seed top-ups are staged to test this.

### 3b. RLPD

| world | reward | dR2D | dH | dDP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| old | sparse | **0.55** | **0.42** | **0.25** |
| old | dense | 0.18 | 0.15 | 0.17 |
| corrected | sparse | — | 0.18 | 0.20 |
| corrected | dense | — | 0.22 | 0.08 |

Two things here are unexplained and registered as open (N10):

1. **The old-world sparse spread is the cleanest source contrast in the project.** dH 0.42 vs dDP
   0.25, on sets that are N-matched (56/56), IC-matched, and length-matched (median 115.0 vs 114.5
   decisions) — so none of the density confounds apply. And it favours *human* demos, the opposite
   of the BC story that started this investigation.
2. **It vanishes in the corrected world** (0.18 vs 0.20), where every cell sits near the floor.
   Candidate explanations — floor compression, the datasets not being the same datasets, or RLPD's
   recipe being mistuned for the new dynamics — are untested. The seed top-ups are the direct test.

### 3c. Mechanism results (solid)

- **The 08-19 dDP_RLPD catastrophe (0/6 seeds, critic → 12,900) was an encoding bug**, not a
  property of model demos: fail tapes written without terminal flags, so the critic bootstrapped
  through states the environment would have terminated at. With terminals labelled, dDP ignites 4/4.
- **DP fail tapes are *sufficient* to damage RLPD** (N5 necessity+sufficiency): adding 8 of them to
  the best set took it 0.55 → 0.15, while adding the same 8 to dDP changed nothing (0.25 → 0.25).
  The mediator is buffer share, not provenance: those tapes are 72% of dR2D's short-episode buffer
  versus 26% of dDP's. Whether a world model cares is the experiment running now.

### 3d. Simulator fidelity (the biggest single improvement)

Replaying the real robot's recorded commands, whole-episode joint tracking error went from
**0.045 → 0.003 rad (p50)** and 0.090 → 0.027 (p95); frames beyond the leash 4.0% → 0.1%. Three
defects, found against real bag data:

| defect | evidence | fix |
|---|---|---|
| No arm gravity compensation | ~4 cm static sag at the extended place pose | `gravity_comp 1.0`, kp ×4 |
| Shelf modelled half-buried (top 6 cm above table; real release heights imply ≥12) | real tool-frame z at release, p50 0.177 base-frame | shelf on the table (`shelf6`) |
| Contact `timeconst` 0.02 s = **8× the engine's stability floor** (`2·substep_dt` = 2.5 ms at substeps 8); penetration ∝ timeconst² | `genesis/options/solvers.py:170`; verified independently | `grasp_timeconst 0.005` |

The first two were **cancelling errors** — fixing only the arm collapsed downstream performance
(nested 20 → 3) because the sag had been compensating for the low shelf. Fixing both gives parity
or better everywhere while tracking the real arm 15× more faithfully.

## 4. The decision in front of us

The contact-timeconstant fix is confirmed **on the cluster** (not just the laptop) as of 23:20:

| | base | +`grasp_timeconst 0.005` |
|---|---|---|
| pick recreation (cluster) | 57/66 | **58/66** — and it rescues uid 246, the knock-over |
| random-teacher control | — | 0/30 ✓ |
| carry penetration (laptop, med-of-max) | 9.9 mm | **1.5 mm** |
| free tip-overs / 75 | 20 | **12** |
| nested / placed_v2 | 17 / 28 | **20 / 31** |
| arm-tracking fidelity | 0.0033 / 0.0285 | 0.0035 / 0.0300 (unchanged) |

**Honest cost:** grip force *rises* 147 → 245 N, because the fingers now stall at the surface
holding full PD error. Every route to lowering it was swept (driver caps, kp, reading remap, pad
friction) and all lose the grasp — this engine has no torsional friction or `impratio` to hold a
low-force pinch. So the grasp is still unphysical, just differently: surface stall instead of 10 mm
of interpenetration. Small unexplained regressions: contact 22 → 20, full-task picked 71 → 69.

**Adopting means rebuilding the demo sets a second time** (~5 h: re-record dH, retrain the DP
teacher, re-record dDP, rebuild sets and lerobot datasets), because 46 of the 48 staged jobs run on
the world this changes. The N5 cells now running are on old-world tapes and are unaffected either
way.

**Recommendation: adopt.** The evidence is cluster-confirmed, it improves the metric we actually
optimise (pick recreation) rather than only the cosmetic one, and paying the rebuild bill now is
cheaper than discovering later that 48 jobs ran on a superseded world. `matched_w3` is not deleted,
so this is reversible.
