## 0. Verdict

1. **The data do not support raising the threshold.** Zero of the 25 tapes the rule fires on
   recover afterwards, at ANY threshold from 45° to 89°. Over all 146 tapes exactly one free
   can returns upright from past 60°, and the rule's existing guard already suppresses that one.
2. **On demonstrations the 60° threshold is very nearly inert.** 21 of the 25 firings land on
   the tape's LAST decision; the rule removes **8 decisions out of 66,055 (0.012 %)** and
   forfeits **0 reward** — no ladder stage ever fires after a trigger on any tipped tape.
3. **What the user is seeing is real, and it is the GUARD, not the threshold.** Six of the 25
   firings happen while the can is still in the gripper (tool-to-can lever 0.002–0.021 m), so
   the frame the chip lights on shows a can being *released while leaning*, not a can lying on
   the shelf. The guard `commanded grip < 0.3` is what lets that through.
4. **The same guard makes the rule MISS more falls than it catches.** 48 tapes contain a free
   horizontal can (≥ 80°, not in hand) during the episode; the rule labels 25 of them. In all
   29 misses the commanded grip was ≥ 0.31 at the moment the can went flat — the fist-push
   posture amendment (p) already documented. `machine 301` lies flat from decision 164 and the
   tape runs 436 more decisions.

And from the rollouts (§8), which answer the two questions the coordinator put on top:

5. **In {RLPD} rollouts the rule fires almost entirely BEFORE the pick** — 19 of 22 firings
   (86 %), at a median decision of **12–21 of 300** — and **never once while the can is in the
   hand**. The in-hand firings of point 3 are a property of human release behaviour, not of
   anything a policy does.
6. **Every one of those 22 cans goes horizontal and is flat at the settle; not one rung is
   granted after the trigger, in either arm.** With the rule off, 25–39 % of all decisions run
   are spent after the can is already dead.

**Recommendation.**
**(a) KEEP TERMINATING** — do not go log-only. Termination is the only channel that tells the
critic a flat can is worth zero (`TIP_PENALTY` is 0, and both `placed_v2` and `nested_v2`
require tilt < 20°, so the ladder is literally unreachable), and it returns the arm to a fresh
start after ~15 decisions instead of 300, which under a decision-counted budget buys roughly an
order of magnitude more grasp attempts.
**(b) KEEP `TIP_DEG = 60.0`** — the data do not support raising it, on demonstrations or on
rollouts.
**(c) KEEP `TIP_PENALTY = 0.0`.**
**(d) REPLACE the guard `a_phys[6] < GRIP_OPEN` with the tracker's `not in_hand`, sustained 4
env frames (1 decision)** — the one change the evidence does support. Measured cost on 146
demonstration tapes: 2 reward, on one tape. §10 and §11.

## 1. The rule, and what "analysis only" means

`baselines/rl/full_env.py` ~1260, constants ~386–401:

```
terminate, info['tipped'] = True   iff   float(a_phys[6]) < GRIP_OPEN (0.3)
                                    and  tilt_deg(can quat) > TIP_DEG (60.0)
```

checked once per **env frame** (4 frames per decision at `action_repeat=4`); `scope='full'` pays
`TIP_PENALTY = 0.0`, so the rule costs nothing but the remainder of the episode. `tilt_deg` is
the angle between the can's body z axis and world z — **it already is the z-axis test**, so
"horizontal" cannot mean a different measurement, only a different threshold on this one.

`baselines/tip_rule_probe.py` disables the rule by assigning `env.TIP_DEG = 1e9` on the env
**instance**. That shadows the class attribute for one object; `FullTaskEnv.TIP_DEG` is never
written, and `build()` asserts it is still 60.0. A second analysis-only override,
`env.terminal_stages = ()`, stops `slide_success` ending a stream before the probe can ask what
happens next. Neither override touches physics — a termination is a label on a frame, not a
force — so every frame before the first would-fire frame is identical to the normal census.

`baselines/annotate_demos.py` gained the same override as `--tip-deg` / `--no-tip`, plus
`--mark-tip`, which watches each env frame for the LIVE rule and marks the decision it would
have fired on. The panel now carries a `can tilt NN.N deg (tip rule: >60 deg AND grip<0.3)`
line and a red `RULE FIRES @dN` tag; the terminal card carries the settled tilt.

resting-band flag. One `genv.end_of_episode()` settle (100 scene steps) runs at the end of every
tape and the settled tilt is recorded.

**Equivalence check (not assumed — measured): 146 / 146 tapes.** For every tape the probe
reconstructs the decision and reason the NORMAL census would have ended on, from the first
would-fire frame and the first `slide_success` frame, and compares it with
`census_{human,machine}.json`. Zero mismatches. So the rule-off trajectories are the census
trajectories, continued.

## 10. Recommendation

**(a0) TERMINATION: keep terminating. Do not make the tip log-only.** This is the coordinator's
top question and the rollouts answer it against log-only, on three grounds.

*The state is genuinely absorbing.* `placed_v2` requires tilt < 20° and `nested_v2` requires it
too, so once the can is flat the ladder is unreachable by construction — not empirically
unlikely, unreachable. §8b measures the consequence directly: across 22 firings in two pilot
policies, **no rung is ever granted after the trigger**, and all 22 cans are flat after the
settle. §3 measures the same on 146 demonstrations: 0 of 25.

*Termination is the only channel that says so.* `TIP_PENALTY` is 0.0, so the tip carries no
reward signal at all; what it carries is `terminated=True`, and a terminated transition's TD
target is `r + 0` instead of `r + γV(s')`. That zero IS the true value of a flat can, supplied
for free. This project already relies on that distinction — `train_rlpd.py`'s
`--demo-terminal-guard` ends every demo tape where the env would have terminated with
`done=True, r=0` and explicitly bootstraps the horizon cap instead, because the unanchored
bootstrap is what sat behind `dDP_RLPD 0/6` (AUDIT_impl F1). Log-only would remove that anchor
and make the critic learn "flat can = 0" from hundreds of discounted steps of nothing.

*Log-only is expensive, and the cost is exploration, not compute.* With the rule off, **25 % of
`dH_s940`'s and 39 % of `dDPfirst_s960`'s decisions are spent after the can is already dead**
(§8b). RLPD's budget is counted in DECISIONS, so those decisions do not buy extra episodes —
they replace them. A tipped episode currently ends at a median decision of 12–21 of 300; ending
it returns the arm to a fresh start roughly fifteen times sooner, which is the opposite of an
exploration cost for a policy that picks on 0–6 of 30 starts. The honest counter-argument — that
early termination denies the learner experience of recovering — is refuted by the same data: no
policy, and no demonstrator, has ever recovered a free can from past 60° in this world.

*If it is ever revisited*, the cheap test is one eval cell per arm with `--tip-deg 1e9`
(`baselines/eval_e2e_tipoff.py`, ~40 s per episode on CPU) plus a short training run; the
prediction to register is that log-only lowers the pick rate per decision spent, because it
spends the budget on dead states.

**(a) Threshold: keep `TIP_DEG = 60.0`. Say plainly that the data do not support raising it.**
Nothing recoverable lives between 60° and 80°: 0 of 25 firings recover at any threshold in
45–89°, and across all 146 tapes only 1 of 54 free crossings of 60° returns upright — a
one-decision transient the current guard already ignores. Moving 60 → 80 changes the label on
2 of 146 tapes (human 262, machine 275) and changes neither outcome. The rollouts agree: of the 22
firings, **14 are already at ≥ 80°** when the rule fires and the other 8 reach ≥ 80° later in
the same episode (22 of 22, §8b), so an 80° rule would fire on exactly the same episodes, a few
decisions later. If
"horizontal" is wanted as a WORD, note that `tilt_deg` already IS the z-axis test (the angle
between the can's body z and world z) and ≥ 80° is the honest reading of it — but adopting 80°
buys a cosmetic relabelling, not a behavioural improvement, and it makes the rule miss two more
falls.

**(b) Guard: replace `float(a_phys[6]) < self.GRIP_OPEN` with the tracker's `not in_hand`,
sustained 4 env frames.** This is the change the evidence actually supports. It removes all six
in-hand firings — the thing the user is seeing and correctly calling wrong — takes the missed
free-horizontal cans from 29 to 2, introduces zero recoveries, and forfeits 2 reward on one
tape (and that +2 is `contact_push` paid for pushing a can that is lying flat, which is its own
bug). `in_hand` is already computed every frame by `StageTracker` and is the same lever test
`nested_v2` uses, so this is one definition serving two predicates rather than a new one. It is
also robust to the open `HELD_LEVER_M` question (0.025 vs Lane 1's 0.030).

**(c) Penalty: keep `TIP_PENALTY = 0.0`.** Nothing here argues for a penalty. Under the new
guard the rule would fire a median of 88 decisions before the end of a tape, so it becomes a
real horizon saving; a penalty on top would punish a state the arm can only reach by attempting
the task, and the ladder already withholds every rung from a flat can.

**(d) If only one thing changes, change the guard, not the threshold.** They are independent:
the threshold moves 2 tapes, the guard moves 27.

**Consequence for scoring, stated before anyone runs it.** `tipped` is one of the three outcomes
`baselines/eval_e2e.py` reports (`TERMINAL_STAGE` / `tipped` / `timeout`, line 330), so it is a
cell in every end-to-end table and in the demonstration census. A change moves prior counts and
the new cells are **not comparable** with cells scored under the old rule:

* threshold 60 → 80 on the demonstration census: `tipped` goes {human} 13 → 12 and {machine}
  12 → 11; human 262 becomes `placed_only`, machine 275 becomes `push_no_nest`.
* guard `grip` → `hand` (sustain 4): tapes terminating on the tip go 25 → 46, and the tapes
  concerned shorten by a median of 88 decisions. **This changes the demonstration sets
  themselves** wherever the relabel path re-executes them, which is a far bigger move than the
  threshold and must be rebuilt-and-re-attested, not patched in place.
* Either way, any table mixing old and new cells has to say which rule produced each row.

## 12. What this does NOT measure

* **Training under a changed rule.** §8 measures two FROZEN checkpoints and the training logs of
  eight runs; it does not measure a policy TRAINED with a different guard. Everything in §10 is
  an argument from the states a current policy reaches, not from a learning curve. The
  amendment's P1–P3 are the registered way to settle it, and they need one arm retrained.
* **Two checkpoints, one learner, one action mode.** §8b is {RLPD}, deterministic, 30 starts per
  arm, final checkpoint. Nothing here is measured on {r2dreamer}, on sampled actions, or at an
  earlier point in training where the tip rate differs (0.13–0.52 across the eight runs). The
  local rollouts also do not bit-reproduce the cluster cells (§8b, provenance caveat).
* **The sparse arm.** The two sparse pilot runs tip least (0.12/0.31) and were not re-rolled.
* **Whether the demonstration sets should be rebuilt** under the new guard. §10's consequence bullet
  says what would move; deciding it is the coordinator's, and it must not happen while the
  `lz_*` pilot is training against the current env.
* **The `contact_push` tilt hole** (human 274, §5) — surfaced here, not investigated.
* **Any threshold below 45°**, which the sweep did not cover because the recovery band
  (30–45°, §6) sits there and a rule inside it would terminate cans that do come back.
