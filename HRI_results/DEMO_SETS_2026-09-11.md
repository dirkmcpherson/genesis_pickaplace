### What the contrast therefore is

Raw human demonstrations versus demonstrations produced by an imitator trained on the pruned,
no-pick-free version of those same demonstrations. The arms differ in source AND in two pruning
effects (idle collapse; the 10 no-pick tapes absent from the teacher's data). {RLPD} and
{r2dreamer} were indifferent to pruning in pick scope (`paper/RESULTS_WM_HUMAN_VS_MACHINE_2026-09-04.md`,
MORNING_TABLE §5); {DP} was not (+0.19 to +0.27). State this as a property of the machine set.

## 2. Phase-scoring method (the "re-execution census")

A demonstration is scored by **replaying its recorded action stream through the training
environment itself** — `FullTaskEnv(scope='full', ladder='staged')`, world
`gc_kp4_riser3_shelf6`, the same code path a training run uses — and reading the stage flags the
environment grants. No classifier is run on the tape; the video annotator and the relabel read
the same flags (`baselines/annotate_demos.py census`, `baselines/rl/relabel_reward.py`; branch
`ladder-unify-2026-09-11`, stamp `full_env=f9e9538d9fc6 genesis_can_env=40544bf73c8c
stage_predicates=de4ffde57cd7`). Flags are sticky; a tape "reaches" a stage if the flag fires at
any decision before the episode ends (the staged ladder terminates on `slide_success` or a tip;
the recorded stream then stops).

| stage | definition (predicate in the env / `baselines/stage_predicates.py`) |
|---|---|
| `picked` | the env's hardened held-can flag |
| `placed_v2` | grip cmd < 0.45 ∧ can in the shelf footprint ∧ z in the resting band ∧ tilt < 20°, sustained 10 env frames |
| `contact` (legacy) | picked ∧ can↔goal solver contact (∧ a wrist clause that is vacuous) — carry-in counts |
| `contact_push` | `placed_v2` granted ∧ can↔goal solver contact ∧ tool on the far side of the can along the can→goal line ∧ no gripper↔goal contact |
| `pushed` | dist_xy(can, goal) decreased ≥ 10 mm cumulatively since the first `placed_v2`, on frames where the can is not in hand |
| `nested_v2` | picked ∧ `placed_v2` granted ∧ dist_xy ≤ 0.081 m ∧ both cans tilt < 20° ∧ z band ∧ not in hand (tool–can lever ≥ 2.5 cm) ∧ at rest (≤ 2 mm over 12 frames) |
| `slide_success` | released (`placed_v2`) ∧ `pushed` ∧ `nested_v2` — the paid terminal |
| `nested_honest` | the post-episode settle (100 scene steps, last command held): picked ∧ dist_xy ≤ 0.081 ∧ both upright — reference only |
| `tipped` | tilt > 60° ∧ grip cmd < 0.3 — terminal (under review, `paper/TIP_RULE_2026-09-11.md`) |

Validation of `nested_v2` against the settle: 60/60 on {RLPD} policy episodes (precision 1.00 /
recall 1.00); on the human tapes precision 1.00 / recall 0.79 (`paper/NESTED_V2_PREDICATE_2026-09-10.md`).
Known reference flaw: the settle nests a can still held in the gripper on 1/146 tapes
(machine 283); `nested_v2` rejects it.

**Hardware caveat.** Re-execution is class-sensitive after contact. The table below is this
box's census; the cluster build of record (64-core nodes, job 3537411) agrees per tape on 68/74
human and 64/72 machine tapes, every disagreement on a tape whose can diverges after contact,
and gives `slide_success` 12 / 13 and `nested_v2` 14 / 15 (this box: 13 / 14 and 14 / 16).
Numbers OF RECORD are the cluster's; the rates here move by ≤ 0.02.

## 4. Open question: sparse or dense reward for the slide

What we want to induce is the human behaviour: release on the shelf, move the gripper to the far
side of the can, push it to settled contact with the goal. The demonstrations reach it by the
slide route 27 times and by a drop 3 times. The question is which reward gets a learner there.

- **Sparse:** pay only the outcome (`nested_v2`, settled contact, +1, terminal). Tests whether
  paying the outcome alone induces the slide. Risk: at a ~1 % occurrence rate in early
  exploration it pays almost never.
- **Staged (current, D2):** picked 1 / placed_v2 1 / release-gated contact_push 2 /
  slide_success 4. Rungs not nested (slide pays without contact_push on ~10/13 human slides:
  arrival is 0.081 m, cans touch at 0.066 m).
- **Nested with a dense slide term (proposed Ladder N, `nested_ramp`):** picked → placed_v2 →
  farside (+1) → slide progress paid per cm of goalward gain from the far side (up to +2, on new
  minima only) → home +4. Every rung requires the previous one. Gives gradient AFTER release,
  which sparse and staged do not.

**Registered test in flight** (PHASE_PLAN amendment (z), pilot jobs 3539249–3539264): staged v
sparse, 2 v 2 seeds per arm per learner, {RLPD} 100k/250k decisions, {r2dreamer} 1M/4M steps.
P7: sparse reaches `nested_v2` in ≥ 1 seed per arm; P8: most policy arrivals come by a drop
(disconfirm = slide route ≥ 50 %). Decision rule: sparse at ≥ the staged rate at the matched
milestone → sparse becomes primary; sparse 0 in all seeds at full budget while staged > 0 →
shaping is necessary. Early read ({RLPD} staged, training rollouts at ~100k, last 100 episodes):
human arm `slide_success` 0.02, `contact_push` 0.02–0.03, `nested_v2` 0.02–0.05; machine arm 0 on
all three; **the tip rule ends 33–52 % of training episodes in every run** — the largest
episode-ending event during learning, ahead of any paid rung. The tip threshold/termination is
under separate measurement (`paper/TIP_RULE_2026-09-11.md`) and is likely the first thing to
change, whichever reward wins.

**What would settle the question:** the pilot's sparse cells at the matched milestones (P7/P8),
the demo-side check of Ladder N (`paper/LADDER_N_DEMO_CHECK_2026-09-11.md`: do the 13 human slides
earn near the maximum under `nested_ramp`?), and the slide smoke test on policy rollouts
(`can_pos_recovery/videos_slide_smoke_2026-09-11/`). Any ladder change is a retrain; none can be
re-scored from checkpoints.
